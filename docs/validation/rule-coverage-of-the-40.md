# How far the rules cover the 40 backtest cases

Written by `scripts/validation/backtest_coverage.py` from the trigger inventory ([`backtest/triggers.json`](../../backtest/triggers.json)) and the hand-entered facts ([`backtest/inputs.json`](../../backtest/inputs.json)). No model, no network, no database. Every case, every trigger and every run is in [`backtest/coverage.json`](../../backtest/coverage.json). Do not edit this file by hand: change the script and run it again.

The 40 cases are 25 decided by the Planning Commission and 15 that did not reach approval (withdrawn or cancelled). Coverage is defined in [`rule-table-extension-spec.md`](rule-table-extension-spec.md), section 5.

## The counts

**Rule coverage.** The rules are run on the facts in the decision record, on the day before filing. A case is fully covered when the rules return every trigger in the inventory with the control the inventory gives at filing, partly covered when they return some, not covered when they return none.

|  | Fully covered | Partly covered | Not covered | No trigger stated in the record |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Every trigger in the inventory**, all 40 cases | 23 | 11 | 6 |  |
| - decided (25) | 15 | 8 | 2 |  |
| - did not reach approval (15) | 8 | 3 | 4 |  |
| **Only triggers a decision document names**, all 40 cases | 22 | 5 | 5 | 8 |
| - decided (25) | 16 | 5 | 4 | 0 |
| - did not reach approval (15) | 6 | 0 | 1 | 8 |

The second count leaves out the triggers the readers inferred from the code (no decision document names them) and the one that is an applicant's claim only. A case with no other trigger is in the last column.

By trigger: the inventory holds 62 (one per family per case, see "How the comparison is made"). The rules return 44. Of the 45 stated in a record they return 34; of the 17 inferred or claimed, 10.

By cause: the inventory counts a hearing by cause once (its counting rule: one cause written in two places of the code). Counted that way the 62 triggers are 54 causes, and the rules return 36 (a pair counts as returned when both halves are). The pairs: N07 (Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1)); N12 (Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1)); D11 (Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1)); D17 (§ 303(e) and § 178(c)(1)); D22 (Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1)); D23 (§ 249.62 and Table 737 Note 7); D24 (§ 303(e) and § 178(c)(1)); D25 (Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1)).

Of the 23 fully covered cases, N05 needed no hearing in the end (approved as a continuation); N08 had nothing to find (principal at filing). Of the 22 fully covered on the triggers a decision document names, N08 had nothing to find (principal at filing); N15 needed no hearing in the end (approved by letter).

**Input coverage.** The same rules on the same dates, run on only what the neutral description states, read the way the product reads a description (through a profile: `facts.concept_facts(facts.profile_from_facts(...))`), with the optional intake questions answered where the case file states the fact (`intake_answers` in the inputs file, applied as the product applies them: `app.memo.intake.apply_intake_answers`, then `normalized()`). 30 cases answer at least one question. The same run without the answers is counted beside it. Fully found means every trigger in the inventory is found with the control at filing.

By trigger, with the intake answers: 32 found, 14 not decided (the rules name the missing input), 11 missed (the rules say nothing), 0 found with a different control, 5 with no rule. The description alone: 28 found, 18 not decided, 11 missed, 0 with a different control, 5 with no rule. The cases whose triggers the answers change: D04, D05, D09, D10, D14. Everything below is the run with the answers.

With the § 102 use handed to the rules by hand (the inputs file's `planning_use`, which the product has no field for): fully found 19, partly 11, none 10; by trigger 35 found, 13 not decided, 8 missed, 1 with a different control. The cases that differ: N03, D05, D14, D24.

**The rules cover it and the input does not:** 12 triggers in 9 cases are returned on the record facts and not found on the description. 5 of those cases are fully covered by the rules. They are listed below.

**Possible false triggers:** on the record facts the rules raise 5 triggers the inventory does not have, in 5 cases. On the description they raise 5, in 5 cases.

**Dating the rules:** read for 2026-11-10 instead of the filing date, at least one trigger reads differently in 14 cases, and the zoning row changes in 9.

**What the zoning row would have read** on the record facts at filing: allowed 2, further review 31, not permitted 3, insufficient information 4. The 2 that read allowed (N01, D16) needed a hearing: in each the only trigger is one with no rule. N01 reads allowed only with the § 102 use stated by hand; without it the row reads insufficient information.

## Cases not fully covered, and why

**Not covered (6):**

- **N01** (C-2, did not reach approval). The only trigger is the unscreened open-air use clause of the Waterfront Special Use District No. 2 (§ 240.2(d); the clause was deleted on 2023-08-28). It was seen once and has no rule. Every built trigger asks for nothing on the record facts, so the zoning row reads allowed where a hearing was required at filing.
- **N11** (NCD-HAIGHT, did not reach approval). An outdoor dining area at the rear of the lot in NCD-HAIGHT. Table 719 makes it conditional unless it keeps within every limit of § 202.2(a)(7). The record never says which limit the patio exceeded, and the record facts do not either, so the rules answer not decided and name the limits. The inventory records conditional because the Commission packet required the hearing.
- **N13** (NCD-LOWER POLK STREET, did not reach approval). The only entry is the applicant's claim (§ 186.1(b): adding a Type 48 licence intensifies an existing use). Planning did not adopt it and approved by letter. The rules do not return it either: the last use was an adult business, which is not a use the rule table carries, so the uses are read as new and both are principally permitted. On this point the rules agree with Planning. They raise use size instead, which Planning did not (see the possible false triggers).
- **N14** (NCD-INNER CLEMENT, did not reach approval). Formula retail is the only ground that fits the section the application cites, and nothing found shows the business is a chain. The record facts leave it unstated, so the rules answer not decided and name it.
- **D16** (NCT-MISSION, decided). The only trigger is the legacy business replacement control of the Mission Street NCT (§ 754(c)(4)). It was seen once and has no rule. On the record facts (a bar after a bar, nothing enlarged) every built trigger asks for nothing, so the zoning row reads allowed where a hearing was required.
- **D19** (NCD-HAIGHT, decided). A restaurant adding alcohol in the Haight Street Alcohol Restricted Use Subdistrict. The rule reads a named licence type (40, 41, 42, 47, 48 or 57). The record says on-sale beer and wine at a bona fide eating place and gives no type number, and the facts objects have no field for what a concept pours, so the record facts hold no licence type. The rules answer not decided and name the licence type. Given beer and wine, they derive Type 41 and return conditional.

**Partly covered (11):**

- **N04** (NCT-FILLMORE, did not reach approval). Formula retail (Table 760) is returned. The second entry is the change-of-operator rule of § 178(c)(3), which the rules print as a note and do not evaluate. At filing both gave conditional, so the row is right.
- **N07** (NCT-MISSION, did not reach approval). A new Restaurant in NCT-MISSION. Table 754 Note 7 caps the eating and drinking uses of the district (167 on the filing date), and a new one is not permitted above the cap. No input carries the count, so the use control is not decided and names the cap. The special district's rule (§ 249.60(f)(1)) is returned as conditional.
- **N15** (NCT-FILLMORE, did not reach approval). The same address as N04, filed after Ord. 62-25 (effective 2025-06-01) took limb (E) out of § 178(c)(3). Formula retail (Table 760) is returned as conditional, which is the ground the application states. The deciding rule was the change-of-operator test, under which Planning approved by letter without a hearing. The rules do not evaluate that test, so their answer here (conditional) is not what happened.
- **D04** (NC-2, decided). A formula retail restaurant enlarged in NC-2 on Chestnut Street. Formula retail is returned: the Taraval Street Restaurant Subdistrict is now placed by its overlay (§ 781.1), and no overlay at this address places it there. Not returned: enlarging an existing conditional use. The existing restaurant is a conditional use only because it is formula retail, and T3 reads the Restaurant row, which is principal (see the points for the rules).
- **D05** (NCD-CASTRO, decided). Returned: Nighttime Entertainment (conditional on the first story, not permitted on the second), the new Bar (conditional) and use size (not permitted from 4,000 square feet). Not returned: the change of a nonconforming use (§ 182(b)). The existing use is a movie theatre, which is not a use the rule table carries, so the trigger for enlarging or intensifying an existing use does not apply.
- **D09** (NCT-MISSION, decided). An outdoor dining area on a parking lot behind a restaurant in NCT-MISSION. The district's own row makes it conditional; § 202.2(a)(7) itself makes it principally permitted in every NCT district within five limits, and the record facts do not say which limit it exceeds, so the rules answer not decided and name the limits. The Commission heard it as conditional.
- **D10** (NCT-MISSION, decided). As D09: an outdoor area behind a restaurant in NCT-MISSION, read against the § 202.2(a)(7) limits that the record facts do not settle.
- **D11** (NCT-MISSION, decided). As N07: a new Restaurant in NCT-MISSION, not decided on the Table 754 Note 7 cap (167 on the filing date). § 249.60(f)(1) is returned.
- **D12** (RM-3, decided). Laundromat removal is returned. The expansion of a nonconforming restaurant in RM-3 is not decided: Table 209.2 has no Restaurant row, the row that governs reads not permitted, and the rules cannot tell whether the use falls under the Limited Commercial Use rows (§§ 186, 186.3). The inventory itself records that neither reader found the clause that makes this expansion conditional.
- **D22** (NCT-MISSION, decided). The new Restaurant is returned under § 249.60(f)(1). The use control of Table 754 is not decided on the Note 7 cap (179 on the filing date): the readers record 148 open in July 2025, but no input carries the count. The storefront merger control (§ 754(c)(2)) was seen once and has no rule. The row is right: further review.
- **D24** (NCD-HAIGHT, decided). Returned: changing the conditions of the 1996 approval, and intensifying an existing use. Not returned: Nighttime Entertainment. The record facts say the entertainment use already exists and continues, and the rules do not apply the nighttime entertainment trigger to a use that continues; they read it under enlarging or intensifying. The motion makes the Table 719 Nighttime Entertainment findings. The row is right: further review.

## Possible false triggers

A trigger the rules raise (conditional or not permitted) that the inventory does not list for the case. "Inventory note" is what the inventory's `considered_not_triggers`, or a settlement note of the record facts, says about the same ground.

**On the record facts:**

| Case | Trigger | Rules | What happened | Inventory note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N10 | T8 removal of a laundromat | conditional | The storefront the bar expands into was a dry cleaner, and the filing date is inside the laundromat rule's period (2021-12-20 to 2024-12-20). ## How the comparison is made

- **Three runs per case**, all through `app.memo.triggers.evaluate`: the record facts on the day before filing; the description's facts on the same day; the record facts on 2026-11-10.
- **The § 102 use.** The record run hands the rules the inputs file's `planning_use` (the decision record classifies the use); the product has no field for it. Through the product's path the record facts give: fully 23, partly 11, not 6; 43 returned of 62. The cases that differ: D05; the zoning row differs in N01 (allowed -> insufficient information). The input run uses the product's path.
- **By cause.** One cause written in two places counts once (triggers.json `notes.counting_rules`): N07, Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1); N12, Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1); D11, Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1); D17, § 303(e) and § 178(c)(1); D22, Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1); D23, § 249.62 and Table 737 Note 7; D24, § 303(e) and § 178(c)(1); D25, Table 754 or 763 and § 249.60(f)(1). Of the special district use control, most items are such mirrors of the district's use control.
- **Families.** New use control of the district -> T1; Use control of a special use district -> T2; Enlarging or intensifying an existing use -> T3; Liquor licence in a restricted district -> T4; Formula retail -> T5; Nighttime entertainment -> T6; Use size -> T7; Laundromat removal -> T8; Outdoor activity area -> T9; Changing the conditions of an earlier approval -> T10; Hours of operation -> T11. Three families were seen once and have no rule: Legacy business replacement (Planning Code § 754(c)(4)); Storefront merger (Planning Code § 754(c)(2)); Unscreened open-air use, Waterfront SUD (Planning Code § 240.2(d)).
- **One item per family per case.** Two entries of one family count once, with the more severe control, which is how the rules combine several readings of one trigger (D05 and D14: first and second story; N09: the formula retail baseline and the district prohibition). The change-of-operator rule (N04, N15) is counted on its own, as no rule: spec section 1a says the rules print it as a note.
- **Controls.** An inventory control written with a qualifier is read by its first word; the full text is kept in the output. Returned means the same control; principal and does not apply count as the same.
- **Site records.** No site record is read. In the product a licence on file at the address can show the existing use; here only the facts object does. Where a description says the space was last used as the use proposed without saying whether that business stays, the use controls answer not decided and name it.
- **District and special districts.** The zoning district is the code at the start of the inventory's district at filing. Each special district the inventory lists is matched by the Planning Code section it names to the product's special use district table. The rows are built from the rule table the product uses, as `tests/test_pathway_cases.py` builds them, and looked up through the file-backed store at one notional point. No control is typed into the script.
- **Not mapped.** Every district of the 40 is in the dated control data and matches the district on the Planning record. Sections the inventory names that the product's table has no row for: § 780.3: North Beach Special Use District: the map has no polygon; the rules key it on the zoning district NCD-NORTH BEACH (triggers.MATCHES), so it is read; § 781.5: Mission Street Formula Retail Restaurant Subdistrict (repealed by Ord. 249-23): not in the product's table; the formula retail trigger says it cannot place an address in it; § 788: Lower Polk Street Alcohol Restricted Use District (expired 2023-06-01): not in the product's table. An entry the inventory places on today's map, or that names no section (a cultural district, a landmark, an area plan), builds no row.
- **Check.** Each run is repeated with only the special district rows a rule reads. The answers are the same in all 40 cases; the script stops if they are not.

## Limits

- The comparison is on controls. A trigger counted as returned gives the inventory's control; the sentence of the code it rests on was not compared with the motion's.
- The facts are hand-entered. Where the record facts leave a fact empty the rules answer not decided, even where the record implies the fact (D19).
- The Historic Buildings rule (§ 202.11, from 2026-04-11) is not evaluated; the rules print a line where it could change an answer.
- Three cases needed no hearing in the end (N05, N13, N15) and one was principal at filing (N08). They are counted by the inventory's trigger entries like every other case. N05: the rules return the North Beach licence trigger (conditional) as the inventory gives it at filing, and the Department then approved the same request without a Conditional Use as a continuation, so the zoning row (further review) is not what happened. In N13, and in N05 and N15, the rules ask for a hearing where none was required in the end (N13 on a trigger outside the inventory, N05 and N15 inside it).
- The not-permitted rows of D05 and D14 are right at filing; the Commission approved those parts under Ord. 225-23 (Eff. 12/10/2023), as the inventory's open_points record.
- Two record facts restate the inventory's own trigger entry rather than the record: D17, changes_existing_business (intensify comes from triggers.json trigger 2; the motion cites §§ 178 and 780.3 and never says intensification); D24, changes_existing_business (intensify comes from triggers.json trigger 3, which the inventory marks derived; the motion does not cite § 178). With them cleared: fully covered 22, partly 12, not 6; 42 returned; enlarging or intensifying returned 2 of 8; possible false triggers on the record 5 in 5 cases. Changing an earlier approval is stated in both motions and is not affected.
